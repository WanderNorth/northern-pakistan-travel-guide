Rent a Car in Skardu: A Practical Guide to Vehicles, Drivers and Road Trips

The lakes, the cold desert and the high plateau are all within reach of Skardu, but only if you have the right vehicle and a realistic plan. Here is how to think it through.

Why transport matters in Skardu

Skardu is a base rather than a single destination. The sights people come for are spread across the valley: lakes on one side, dunes on another, a high plateau further up. Public transport between them is limited, so most visitors arrange their own vehicle for at least part of the trip.

Distances look short on a map, but mountain roads are slow. A drive of a few dozen kilometres can take a good part of a morning, and conditions can change after rain or a landslide. Choosing the right vehicle and the right kind of arrangement helps you spend more time at the viewpoint and less time worrying about the road.

This guide covers the main decisions: whether to take a driver, which kind of vehicle suits your route, what a day trip involves, and what to check before you commit.

Good to know

  • Shangrila Lake is roughly 20 km from Skardu town
  • Deosai National Park sits on a plateau averaging about 4,100 m
  • Road conditions can change quickly with weather
  • High routes are usually open for a short summer season
  • Fuel and repair options thin out away from town

With a driver or self-drive?

Both are possible. The right choice depends on your experience, your route and how much you want to manage yourself.

Hiring with a local driver

A local driver knows the road surface, the slow corners and the places where rockfall is common. You can concentrate on the scenery, and a driver can often suggest timings and stops that suit the light and the weather. This is the option many first-time visitors choose.

Driving yourself

Self-drive gives flexibility, but it suits experienced drivers who are comfortable on narrow, unpaved mountain roads with steep drops. Check licence and insurance requirements, and agree clearly with the owner who is responsible for damage and breakdowns.

A mix of both

Some travelers use easy roads around town on their own and book a driver for demanding days such as the high plateau. This keeps costs flexible while putting experience where it matters most.

Choosing the right vehicle

Match the vehicle to the roads you plan to take, not just to the number of passengers.

Saloon car

Economical and comfortable for paved routes around town and towards the Kachura lakes. Low ground clearance makes it a poor match for rough tracks and high-altitude roads.

SUV or 4x4

Higher clearance and better grip for gravel, stream crossings and rough sections. Many travelers choose this type for Deosai and other high routes.

Van or coaster

Sensible for families and groups sharing one vehicle and one itinerary. Check that the route you want is suitable for a larger vehicle before you book.

Day trips by road from Skardu

Typical outings, with the kind of road you can expect. Always check current conditions before you set off.

Shangrila and Kachura lakes

A short drive out of town on the Skardu-Khaplu road. Suitable for most vehicles in good weather, and a relaxed first day while you adjust to the altitude.

Sarfaranga Cold Desert

Close to town, with sand dunes and mountain views. Go early or late in the day for softer light and cooler temperatures.

Deosai National Park

A high plateau with a short visiting season and a rough road. A high-clearance vehicle and an early start are commonly recommended, along with warm layers and spare food and water.

Shigar Valley

A scenic drive to historic buildings and villages. Allow a full day, with time to walk around rather than just photograph from the road.

Arranging your vehicle

Compare a few options before you decide. Ask what the price includes, how many days the vehicle is available, whether a driver, fuel and tolls are covered, and who pays if the route changes because of weather.

If you prefer to organise everything in one place, you can look at Rent a Car in Skardu options alongside tour planning, so that transport and itinerary fit together from the start.

Whichever provider you use, get the main terms confirmed in writing, and share your planned route so the vehicle you are offered matches the roads you will actually drive.

Questions to ask first

  • Is a driver included, and for how many hours each day?
  • Is fuel included or charged separately?
  • What happens if a road is closed on the day?
  • Is the vehicle suitable for the high routes I want?
  • What are the cancellation and change terms?

Before you hit the road

A short checklist that prevents the most common problems.

  • Vehicle condition: look at tyres, brakes, lights and the spare wheel, and make sure tools are on board.
  • Documents: carry identification, the vehicle paperwork and any permits your route requires.
  • Fuel: fill up in town before heading out, as stations are fewer on high routes.
  • Weather and roads: check conditions the evening before and again in the morning.
  • Timing: start early and aim to be off high roads well before dark.
  • Supplies: carry water, snacks, warm clothing and a charged phone or power bank.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to hire a vehicle with a driver?

For first-time visitors this is often the easier option, because the driver knows the roads and conditions. Self-drive can work for experienced mountain drivers on simpler routes.

Do I need a 4x4 in Skardu?

Not for every route. Paved roads around town and to the Kachura lakes suit most vehicles. For rough high-altitude roads such as Deosai, a high-clearance vehicle is commonly preferred.

How early should I start a day trip?

Early starts help you avoid afternoon weather changes and give you a buffer for slow sections. Plan to finish the most demanding stretches well before dark.

What if a road is closed or the weather turns bad?

Closures can happen after heavy rain, snow or landslides. Keep a spare day in your schedule and ask in advance how changes to the plan are handled.

When is the best time to travel by road?

Late spring to early autumn is the usual window, when most routes are open. High roads have a shorter season, so confirm current conditions close to your dates.
